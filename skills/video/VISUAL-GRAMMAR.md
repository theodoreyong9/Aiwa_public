# Visual grammar: choosing and staging the elements with intelligence

What a Claude Code session reads at stage 3 of `PROMO-VIDEO.md`, before writing `video.html`. Elements chosen by habit (planets for « worlds », a phone for « app ») make a film that looks like every film. Write the grammar first, in `grammar.md`.

## The five parts of `grammar.md`
1. **The central metaphor.** What does the *structure* of the subject look like? (Aiwa: a small light that wakes when spoken to, and lights what it touches.) Everything on screen is that metaphor, seen from closer or farther.
2. **The cast.** Every element (character, object, place, light, sound) in a row: *what it means · how it moves · what it can touch*. An element with no meaning is cut.
3. **The rules of contact.** At least one contact in every two shots: two elements that change each other (the voice makes the ember flare; the angel's light reaches a world and the world answers; the thing built appears in the phone and in the room). Elements that never touch are a slide show of props.
4. **The hero set.** One place or object that returns, changed (the ember: asleep, awake, shared). A film with six unrelated sets is six films.
5. **The catalogue check.** For each real thing, search `asset-research` (CC0 models, lights, textures) before drawing it; write down why a thing is procedural. Anything photographic (a person, a place) is footage or the user's generator, never a drawing passed off as real.

## Three tests, applied to each shot
- **Swap test**: replace the main object by another; if the shot reads the same, the object carries no meaning.
- **Touch test**: does something in the shot affect something else?
- **Light test**: whose light is it? A light with no source in the story is decoration. In a film of one ember, the ember is the key light: faces, hands and worlds are lit *by it*, and its colour is the grade.

## Staging
Foreground, middle, background in every 3D shot (parallax and focus need them); one move per shot, different from the one before; the eye is led by light and by motion, never by a caption; type sits where the picture is empty, and moves with the picture, not over it; the element that matters is never the smallest thing in the frame.

## Atmosphere (cheap and effective without a GPU)
Dust and sparks (emissive beads with slow drift); a soft glow card behind every bright part (a plane with a radial gradient: no compositor needed); depth of field on the 3D camera; a faint haze as a graded backdrop; a floor reflection blurred, not mirror-sharp; a dark ground with one warm and one cool accent at most.
