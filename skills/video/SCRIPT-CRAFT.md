# Script craft: how to write what the film says and shows

What a Claude Code session reads at stage 2 of `PROMO-VIDEO.md`, before writing `script.md`. A script that restates the brief line by line is a brochure read aloud. This file is the method that stops that, and a **gate** (below) that catches it before one frame is rendered.

## The engine of a short film: one person, one want, one obstacle, one turn, one payoff
- **A person** the viewer can picture (not « users »): who, where, when, what they say. A scenario is allowed as an *illustration* (said so on screen and in the report); it is never a testimonial, a number or a result nobody measured. It may only show what the creation really does (facts.md).
- **A want and an obstacle**, both ordinary: « I have an idea, and no time, no tools ». The obstacle is the true tension of the brief.
- **The turn**: the moment the creation changes the situation, visible in one image and at most one line. The product is the *turn*, never the *subject*: the film is not about Aiwa, it is about the moment a sentence becomes a thing.
- **The payoff**: what the person has afterwards, shown, not claimed. Then one action.
Write these five in five lines at the top of `script.md` (the story), before any voice-over. If you cannot, the idea is not ready: go back to the brief.

## Method
1. **The human moment.** From facts.md only, choose the smallest concrete moment that carries the message (a night, a kitchen table, one sentence spoken to a phone). Name the person, the place, the hour, the exact words they say.
2. **The motif.** One image or sound that returns three times and *changes* (asleep, awake, shared): the film's spine, and the thing sound and light are built on.
3. **The line budget.** The voice speaks at most 40 % of the time. A line starts *after* the picture has shown it. Each line **does** something (asks, commands, answers, promises): never describes the picture. When there is a human moment, use **two voices**: the person's, plain and short, and the product's, in two to five words (« Done. »).
4. **Specific beats general.** Replace every adjective by something seen or heard. « Powerful, simple, free » becomes the thing that is free, shown. Numbers only if true.
5. **Escalate.** Each beat is bigger, closer, or faster than the one before; one reversal in the middle (what the viewer expected is wrong); the loudest moment is not the first one.
6. **The cut test.** Strike each line in turn: if the film is the same without it, it goes. Read the whole thing aloud, timed: about 150 words a minute in English, and a film of 50 s carries 60 to 90 words, not 130.
7. **End on an action** (what to do, where, for how much), held long enough to read, after a breath of silence.

## Signs of a brochure (rewrite when you see two)
Features as nouns in a row · « and » chains · superlatives nobody proved · no person · every scene the same length · the end is a slogan · the first picture is a logo · the voice never stops.

## The gate: a script critique before any render (minutes, not hours)
A fresh agent (no code, no brief of yours, no pictures) reads only `script.md` and answers: *Who is the person? What do they want? What turns, and in which image? What would you remember tomorrow? Where did you get bored? What is invented?* Score 1 to 5 on **story**, **specificity**, **turn**, **economy**, **end**. Anything under 4: rewrite and ask again, at most three rounds. Only then stage 3. Write the questions, the answers and the scores in `critique-script.md`.
