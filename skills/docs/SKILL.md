# Documents

A letter, a CV, a report, a proposal, a spreadsheet.

- Formats: text → `.pdf` to read, `.docx` to edit; a table → `.xlsx`. When she names none: `.pdf`.
- A clear structure: a title, short sections, no filler. One idea per paragraph.
- When she gives you a document of hers, read all of it, and use only what it says: invent no position, figure, client or price.
- Look at the result before publishing: open the produced file, convert a page to an image, and read it as she will.
- Publish with the tag `docs-…`.

## Starting from a file of hers

When she gives you a document (a CV, a letter, a sales pitch, a brochure, a presentation, notes), read ALL of it, say in one sentence (in
the notes of the release) what kind it is, and shape what you make to that kind: a CV gives a path (who she is, what she can do, the proof,
how to reach her); a sales pitch gives a sale (the problem, the solution, the proof, the price, the call to action); for any other kind,
choose the form that serves the purpose.

Use only what the file says: invent no position, figure, client or price, and keep apart, in a `facts.md` next to your sources, what comes
from the file and what you add. Her work on GitHub is used ONLY when the request says so: then take, among her PUBLIC repositories, those
that serve the purpose, and never a private repository she did not name.

Read each source as it is: a CSV, a JSON or a text directly; an `.xlsx` with Python (openpyxl or pandas); a PDF with `pdftotext`; an image
or a video by citing or embedding it, saying nothing about its content that you could not read.
